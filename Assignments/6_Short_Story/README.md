# Large Language Models Often Say One Thing and Do Another

## About the Research

This research paper explores a critical issue in assessing the reliability of Large Language Models (LLMs): **the consistency between their words and deeds**. As LLMs become central to various applications and interact with diverse user populations, ensuring their reliable and consistent performance is crucial.

### Key Highlights:

- **Problem Addressed**: The paper investigates whether LLMs actually behave in ways consistent with what they claim or state in their responses
- **Novel Benchmark**: Introduces the **Words and Deeds Consistency Test (WDCT)** - a quantitative evaluation framework
- **Testing Domains**: Establishes strict correspondence between word-based and deed-based questions across multiple domains:
  - Opinion vs. Action
  - Non-ethical Value vs. Action
  - Ethical Value vs. Action
  - Theory vs. Application
- **Key Finding**: The evaluation reveals **widespread inconsistency** between words and deeds across different LLMs and domains
- **Experimental Approach**: Conducted experiments with both word alignment and deed alignment to observe their mutual impact

This research is important for understanding LLM reliability, especially as these models become more integrated into real-world applications where consistency between stated values and actual behavior is critical.
